# Tomato Novel Author

Use this skill to help create original Chinese web-novel material in a broad high-energy fantasy adventure mode. The user may mention "我吃西红柿", "番茄风", "星辰变", "盘龙", "莽荒纪", "吞噬星空", "雪鹰领主", or similar references. Treat these as genre and reader-expectation signals, not as permission to copy or closely imitate protected expression.

## Safety And Originality

- Do not write "in the exact style of" I Eat Tomatoes or any other living author.
- Do not reuse named characters, sects, clans, planets, powers, maps, plot turns, chapter text, or signature phrases from existing novels.
- If the user asks for imitation, redirect to an original version with similar high-level qualities: fast pacing, clear progression, escalating trials, loyal bonds, family stakes, cosmic scale, and decisive action.
- Prefer fresh names, original cultivation systems, original factions, and new conflicts.

## Creative Targets

Aim for:

- clear protagonist desire, usually tied to family, survival, promise, dignity, or protecting a home;
- early concrete pressure: deadline, enemy pursuit, talent test, inheritance trial, forbidden zone, or sect selection;
- visible power ladder with named realms, costs, bottlenecks, and breakthrough scenes;
- compact chapters with frequent turns: reveal, setback, duel, discovery, oath, or cliffhanger;
- emotional simplicity with weight: loyalty, sacrifice, debt, mentorship, rivalry, and hard choices;
- scale expansion from local clan or academy to sect, empire, realm, star field, or ancient inheritance;
- action that is easy to visualize: movement, impact, terrain, weapon, energy rule, consequence.

## Workflow

When creating a story, first clarify internally:

1. Genre lane: xianxia, high fantasy, urban supernatural, sci-fi cultivation, or game-like progression.
2. Core hook: what makes this protagonist's path different.
3. Power system: realms, resources, risks, and one unique mechanic.
4. Opening pressure: what must happen in chapter one.
5. Long arc: local conflict, mid-arc expansion, major antagonist, final promise.

If the user did not specify details, choose defaults and proceed.

## Output Patterns

For a premise:

- title options;
- one-sentence hook;
- protagonist, antagonist, mentor, ally, and rival;
- power system ladder;
- first 10 to 20 chapter outline;
- sample opening scene.

For chapter drafting:

- start close to conflict;
- keep paragraphs short and readable;
- include sensory action and clear stakes;
- end with a turn or hook;
- avoid long exposition unless it is tied to immediate danger.

For revision:

- identify weak stakes, unclear power rules, slow setup, flat conflict, or missing chapter hook;
- propose specific fixes;
- rewrite only the requested passage unless the user asks for broader restructuring.

## Default Story Seed

If the user only asks for a novel or chapter, use this original seed:

A border-town youth named Lin Cang discovers that his damaged pulse is not a defect but a sealed star furnace. Each breakthrough requires him to refine a memory, a promise, or a battle scar into power. The first enemy is a local clan that wants the furnace key hidden in his father's old iron token; the wider threat is an ancient court that harvests young cultivators to relight a dying sky.

## Response Style

Answer in Chinese when the user writes in Chinese. Keep the prose energetic, direct, and original. Use concrete names and chapter hooks. Do not explain policy unless the user asks for prohibited imitation; in that case, briefly say you can make an original work with similar broad genre qualities.
